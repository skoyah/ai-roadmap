Seven ways this goes wrong

Each one is common enough to be predictable.

  1. Framework shopping. Learning LangGraph before you've written a raw agent loop means you'll spend your time debugging the framework instead of the problem.
  2. Skipping evals. You'll ship something that demos beautifully and fails in week three, with no idea why — and you'll be tempted to fix it by editing the prompt at random.
  3. Agents where a workflow would do. More autonomy isn't more sophistication. It's more variance and more cost.
  4. Chasing model releases. The models improve on their own. Your context pipeline, evals and guardrails are what compound.
  5. Measuring the wrong thing. Any metric that rewards volume of AI-generated output will reliably produce volume of AI-generated output.
  6. Treating governance as later. Retrofitting audit trails onto forty departmental automations is a far worse job than paving the road first.
  7. Learning alone. Your advantage over an AI-native new hire is that you know your company's systems, data and politics. Pair with the people you're enabling.

How this roadmap was built, and what the review pass changed

Drafting panel: an LLM systems engineer, an internal-platform and enablement lead, a developer-experience engineer focused on AI in the SDLC, an AI security specialist, and a learning designer.

Review panel: a staff-level AI engineer, a skeptic, and an adoption/change-management lead.

  • Cut all model training and math prerequisites. Not your job, and the most common way this kind of roadmap gets bloated. Fine-tuning is deliberately absent — with modern context windows and good retrieval it's rarely the right first answer.
  • Cut "learn Python properly" to "learn to read Python." Contested by one reviewer; the majority view was that TypeScript is where your ecosystem leverage is.
  • Promoted evals from a late-stage topic to something starting in week three that never ends. The first draft had it at the end — precisely how everyone gets it wrong.
  • Promoted security out of an appendix. Since your team will hand agent-building tools to non-engineers, the lethal trifecta has to be a design constraint from day one.
  • Made the workflow-before-agent bias explicit, with a written comparison as a deliverable.
  • Added the compaction and prompt-caching point in Phase 3 — recent, counterintuitive, and it saves real money.
  • Added measurement and adoption to Phases 6 and 7. The reviewer's note: "the technical parts of this job are the easy half; the roadmap was 90% technical and that ratio is a lie."
  • Flagged that spec-driven tooling is strongest on greenfield work. Going into an existing PHP codebase, adopt progressively and expect friction.
